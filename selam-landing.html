06 · Why the giants can't build this

The big labs can't follow her here.

Not "haven't gotten around to it yet" — can't. Selam's biggest advantages are structural. They come from running on hardware you own, with keys you own, on a price the subscription labs can't copy without breaking their own business.

a

iMessage requires your Mac

There is no iMessage API. Cloud services that fake one rack Macs in data centers and get banned. The only legitimate way for an agent to reach iMessage is to run on a Mac you own — which is exactly, and only, where Selam lives.

b

The clouds got evicted

Meta banned general-purpose assistants from WhatsApp — ChatGPT was out by January 2026. Selam isn't a bot platform reaching in from outside. She sends from your own accounts, on your own machine, as you.

c

Their pricing can't do this

A heavily-used $200/mo agent plan costs its lab thousands in compute — that's why every one of them is metered, capped and throttled. Pay once, bring your own keys, run her all night. They can't match that without cannibalizing themselves.

07 · Guardrails

Autonomy you can walk away from.

Autonomous agents made headlines this year for the wrong reasons — thousands of instances left wide open on the internet, agents burning budgets, agents claiming work they never did. Selam was engineered the opposite way: local, capped, tiered, and verified.

a

Nothing listening on the internet

Selam runs on your Mac and talks outward. No public ports, no hosted dashboard, no exposed instance for someone to find. There's nothing to break into because nothing is listening.

b

Four trust tiers, T0–T3

Every action is classified before it runs. Silent internal work at T0; anything irreversible or public — sending money, posting, emailing a human — sits at the top and waits for your spoken yes.

c

Spend caps at four levels

Per attempt, per run, per task per day — and a single kill switch over all autonomous work. A runaway loop hits a wall in cents, not dollars.

d

Fails safe, automatically

An autonomous task that fails three runs in a row pauses itself and tells you — it doesn't keep hammering, and it doesn't quietly disappear.

e

Verifies her own work

Deliverables pass mechanical checks before you ever see them — a research digest without real sources gets flagged as unverified, not read to you as fact.

f

Never claims what she didn't see

A send only counts when it's observed. If a message can't be confirmed, she says exactly that. Every autonomous run leaves a plain-file record on your disk you can audit.

Questions you're probably asking

The honest FAQ.

Do I need technical skills to set this up?+
You need to be comfortable creating an account on an AI provider (like Anthropic or OpenAI) and pasting an API key into a settings screen — or just sign in with Google or OpenRouter and skip keys entirely. The first-run flow walks you through it. If you can set up a Gmail account, you can set up Selam.
What's the real total cost?+
Selam is $10 to try for 30 days, then $89 more to own it forever — $99 total ($99 outright with no trial). That's it from us. On top of that you pay AI providers directly: your chosen brain model and her voice (OpenAI voices cost pennies per conversation; ElevenLabs optional from $5/mo). The avatar runs entirely on your Mac — no avatar subscription, ever. Use the calculator above to dial in your number.
What does "own it forever" actually mean?+
You own a perpetual license — no expiration, no forced upgrades. $10 tries Selam for 30 days with full access; $89 more (or $99 outright) makes her yours forever. First 12 months of updates are free; after that, staying current is an optional $99/yr update pass — and the version you own keeps working either way. When the mobile companion ships, its hosted relay will be a small subscription called Selam Plus, which will never gate anything that runs on your Mac.
Can you see my conversations?+
No. Your keys live in your macOS Keychain. Conversations go straight from your Mac to your chosen AI providers. The avatar runs on-device — Selam's face never leaves your Mac. We don't have a server in the loop. We never see the content — we don't want to.
Is it safe to let it act on my behalf?+
Every action that touches the outside world — sending a message, making a purchase, deleting a file — goes through an approval gate. You see what it plans to do and click approve. Over time you can set policies. Trust is built, not assumed.
She has a crypto wallet — is that safe?+
It ships switched off; nothing exists until you enable it. When you do, keys are generated on your Mac and stored in your Keychain — we never see them, and there's no server custody because there's no server. Receiving is just an address and a QR. Sending is fenced three ways: every transfer needs your spoken approval (no trust rule or setting can skip it — that's enforced in code), amounts are hard-capped per transaction and per day, and she can never send to an address she wasn't given by you. You also get a standard 12-word recovery phrase — revealed only behind Touch ID — that restores the wallet in any mainstream wallet app. Treat it like a pocket, not a vault.
If I stop using it, do I keep my agent's memory?+
Completely. Your identity files (SOUL, IDENTITY, MEMORY, USER) are plain Markdown in your home directory — not locked in any cloud. And when you want all of it in one place, say "export everything you know" — or click Settings → Data → Export everything — and she packages every memory, conversation and work product into a single archive (your API keys are never included). Walk away and take it all with you; come back and pick up where you left off. It's your agent's DNA, and you own it.
Windows? iPhone? Linux?+
macOS today (14+, Apple silicon recommended). Windows in Q3. Mobile is under design as a companion app after the desktop experience stabilizes. Linux is on the list for later.
Why is it called Selam?+
Selam means "peace." Our founder's agent is named Selam — she was the first, and proved the concept. Your agent will have their own name.
